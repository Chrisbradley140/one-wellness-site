Their story

Two careers at the top of world sport. One decision.

Aaron and Bianca Cook spent more than two decades in the hardest rooms in taekwondo — Olympic medals, world titles, world No.1 rankings. Then they chose Riyadh, and a very different kind of work.

Elite sport teaches you one thing above all: almost nothing about progress is dramatic.

It is the same session, done properly, on the days you feel like it and the days you don't. Two decades of that is what Aaron and Bianca actually bring to a client — not the medals, but the method underneath them.

The years of being watched

Arenas, cameras, national kit. Every session measured against the next opponent.

Why Riyadh

They came for the sport. They stayed for the city.

Aaron competed at the Saudi Games in Riyadh in 2023. What they found was a city rebuilding itself at speed — and a lot of people who wanted to train properly but had nowhere sensible to do it.

So they stopped competing and started coaching. Not in a gym with a hundred members, but privately, in people's own spaces, a small number of clients at a time.

We are on a mission to make the people of Riyadh fitter, healthier and happier.

What carries over

What two decades of competing actually teaches you.

Patience

Nobody peaks in six weeks. Programmes are built in blocks and judged over months, which is why they hold.

Precision

A coach who spent twenty years being corrected knows exactly what to look for — and what to leave alone.

Perspective

They have trained at the limit, so they know you don't need to. Hard enough to work, sustainable enough to keep.
